## 2. Technical claims

### Claim 1: Kloppers & Kröger (2005) IJHMT Poppe equations and Bošnjaković Lewis factor
**VERIFIED with a caveat (checked against the authors' companion paper; the IJHMT full text is paywalled and was not read).**
The open-access paper by the same authors (Kloppers & Kröger, "Cooling tower performance: a critical evaluation of the Merkel assumptions", *R&D Journal* 20(1), 2004 (equations on p. 25), https://scielo.org.za/pdf/rd/v20/06.pdf, p. 25) prints the following:
- Eq. (4)/(5): `dw/dTw = c_pw(w_sw−w)m_w/m_a / [i_masw−i_ma + (Le_f−1){i_masw−i_ma−(w_sw−w)i_v} − (w_sw−w)c_pw T_w]` and `di_ma/dTw = c_pw(m_w/m_a)[1 + (w_sw−w)c_pw T_w/[...]]`.
- Eq. (7) gives dMe_P/dTw.
- Eqs. (9), (10) and (12) are the supersaturated forms, with denominator `i_masw−i_ss + (Le_f−1){i_masw−i_ss−(w_sw−w_sa)i_v+(w−w_sa)c_pw T_w} + (w−w_sw)c_pw T_w`.
- Eq. (6): `Le_f = 0.865^(2/3) [ (w_sw+0.622)/(w+0.622) − 1 ] / ln[(w_sw+0.622)/(w+0.622)]`. Eq. (11) is the same with `w_sa` in place of `w` for supersaturated air.

All of these match METHODS §3.6 term for term. The paper attributes the Lewis-factor relation to Bošnjaković and says it was used by Poppe & Rögener. The exponent is printed as 2/3; METHODS writes 0.667, so 2/3 is preferable. The IJHMT 2005 paper is widely reported to present the same set of equations, but I could not open it to confirm its equation numbers. Before citing IJHMT for specific equations, check them in the publisher PDF, or cite the open-access R&D Journal paper as well.

### Claim 2: Braun, Klein & Mitchell (1989) effective saturation state
**VERIFIED (secondary: course text by Mitchell & Braun; the original ASHRAE paper was not accessed).**
Mitchell & Braun's text, Ch. SM 3 "Cooling Towers" (Purdue ME418 supplement, URL in table row 3), cites Braun et al. (1989). It states: "it is assumed that the humidification of the air stream in the tower is equivalent to that of an air stream that passes over a wetted surface at a uniform temperature… (3.24) … The air outlet humidity ratio is calculated assuming that the evaporation is from this saturated surface (3.25) where w_s,eff is the saturation humidity ratio at the enthalpy h_s,eff". Its worked example spells these out: `h_s_eff = h_a_in + (h_a_out − h_a_in)/(1 − exp(−Ntu))` and `w_a_out = w_s_eff + (w_a_in − w_s_eff)*exp(−Ntu)`. These match METHODS §3.5.

### Claim 3: Henderson-Sellers (1984) λ = 1.91846×10⁶ [T/(T−33.91)]² J/kg
**VERIFIED (secondary; the paper's full text is paywalled).** ScienceDirect Topics, "Heat of Vaporization", says: "Henderson-Sellers provided the following equation for the latent heat of vaporization (J/kg), valid from the melting point to 323 K: ΔHv = 1.91846×10⁶ (T/(T−33.91))²", with T in K (https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/heat-of-vaporization). The stated validity range (up to 323 K) should be mentioned if the formula is used above 50 °C.

### Claim 4: Fournier (1977) amorphous silica, t = 731/(4.52 − log S) − 273.15
**VERIFIED.** USGS Open-File Report 82-308 (Rapport, 1982, p. 4, https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1982/0308/report.pdf) says: "The equations used in the program are taken from Fournier (1977) … Amorphous silica TAM = 731/(4.52 − log SiO2) − 273.15 … All concentrations are in mg/l or ppm and results are given in degrees C." This rearranges exactly to log S = 4.52 − 731/T.
Caveats:
- Fournier's units are mg/kg (≈ mg/L), as METHODS uses.
- Fournier's expressions are fits along the vapour-saturation curve for geothermal temperatures. Applying them at 20–35 °C may be outside the calibrated range, which I did not verify. Check the original paper and say this in the paper.

### Claim 5: "Evaporation ≈ 0.00085 × gpm × range(°F)"
**VERIFIED in wording; edition UNCERTAIN.** Perry's Handbook, Section 12 (cooling-tower "Makeup water" subsection): "Evaporation loss can be estimated by W_e = 0.00085 W_c (T1 − T2) (12-14c), where W_c = circulating water flow, m³/h or gal/min at tower inlet, and T1 − T2 = inlet water temperature minus outlet water temperature, °F. The 0.00085 evaporation constant is a good rule-of-thumb value."
- This text was read only through an unofficial online copy of Section 12 and secondary pages that cite Perry's. The edition (7th 1997 or 8th 2008) and page number were not confirmed.
- No Marley/SPX or CTI primary source with the 0.00085 constant was found. Marley's "Cooling Tower Fundamentals" was not reached.

### Claim 6: EnergyPlus I/O Reference, Evaporation Loss Factor 0.15–0.27 %/K, citing Qureshi & Zubair
**Partly verified; the citation part FAILED.**
- Range verified. EnergyPlus v22.2.0 I/O Reference (`doc/input-output-reference/src/overview/group-condenser-equipment.tex`, CoolingTower:* objects) says: "The value entered here is in units of percent-per-degree Kelvin… Typical values are from 0.15 to 0.27 [percent/K]. The default is 0.2." The IDD gives `\units percent/K`, `\default 0.2`.
- **The I/O Reference cites no source for this range.** Qureshi & Zubair do not appear in the cooling-tower text. EnergyPlus cites Qureshi & Zubair only in the source code `EvaporativeFluidCoolers.cc`, and the paper it cites is *Prediction of evaporation losses in evaporative fluid coolers*, Appl. Therm. Eng. 27 (2007) 520–527, not the 2006 HTE paper. There it computes a *default* factor for the EvaporativeFluidCooler objects as `(113 − 8.417 RH + 1.6147 T_db)×1e-5`. **METHODS §3.3 should drop the attribution "(citing Qureshi & Zubair)".**
- **Side finding (units bug).** In `CondenserLoopTowers.cc` (v22.2.0 and current `develop`), the code reads `UserEvapLossFactor = NumArray(19)` with no `/100`. It then computes `EvapVdot = UserEvapLossFactor * (T_in − T_out) * (m_w/ρ)`. So a documented "percent/K" value of 0.2 is applied as a *fraction* per K, which is 20 %/K, a factor of 100 too high. Drift, by contrast, is converted with `/100.0`. The fluid-cooler correlation returns a fraction (about 0.0012–0.0016/K), so it is consistent with the code but not with the documented units. Community Q&A (unmethours.com/questions/29471) reports the same behaviour: users must enter 0.0015–0.0027. This could not be confirmed by running EnergyPlus here. It is worth adding to METHODS §8 and `bugreports/`, and the `LF` method comparison should state which interpretation it emulates.

### Claim 7: Langelier pHs = (9.3 + A + B) − (C + D) with the A–D constants
**NOT VERIFIED as "Standard Methods".**
- The A–D form and constants (A = (log TDS − 1)/10, B = −13.12 log(°C + 273) + 34.55, C = log[Ca as CaCO₃] − 0.4, D = log[alk as CaCO₃]) are widely reproduced, for example in US patents and water-treatment course notes (https://water.mecc.edu/courses/ENV115/LSI.htm). Secondary sources attribute them to the Carrier *Handbook of Air Conditioning System Design* (McGraw-Hill, 1965), but I could not see that book's text.
- Standard Methods 2330 (doi:10.2105/SMWW.2882.024) computes pHs from equilibrium constants (pK₂, pK_s) with activity corrections, not from this empirical A–D formula. "Constants as in Standard Methods" is therefore very likely inaccurate.
- Recommendation: cite Langelier (1936) for the index, and cite the A–D form as an empirical approximation (Carrier Air Conditioning Co., 1965), checked against a physical copy. Alternatively, compute pHs by the SM 2330 method.
- Note also that B uses T + 273 in the usual statement; METHODS uses 273.15, a negligible difference.

### Claim 8: CTI ATC-105 4-point Chebyshev at 0.1, 0.4, 0.6, 0.9 of range
**Partly verified (secondary only); ATC-105 text not accessible (paywalled).**
- Several sources state that CTI adopts the four-point Tchebycheff integration for KaV/L. For example, "a four-point Tchebycheff integration formula … is adopted by the Cooling Technology Institute for its testing protocol" (UTM *Jurnal Teknologi*, https://journals.utm.my/jurnalteknologi/article/view/20849). A calculator page attributes the method to CTI ATC-105 (2019).
- The node positions 0.1/0.4/0.6/0.9 with equal weights were seen only in secondary or software sources. They are the standard textbook statement, but I did not confirm them in ATC-105.
- Recommendation: write "Chebyshev four-point rule as used in CTI practice (CTI ATC-105)" and confirm the nodes against the code, or cite a textbook that prints them.
